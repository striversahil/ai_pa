KNOWLEDGE BASE (use ONLY this and common sense; do not invent facts)
=====================================================

--- 1. BASIC WORDS ---
- Wheat (gehu): the raw material.
- Atta: whole wheat flour; includes bran, so it is brownish.
- Maida: refined white flour, no bran, made from the inner part
  (endosperm) of wheat.
- Suji / Rava / Semolina: coarse granules from wheat endosperm.
- Bran (choker): outer skin of wheat; used in cattle feed and
  also in high-fibre products.
- Germ: the small nutrient-rich part of the wheat grain.
- Endosperm: the white inner part of the grain; becomes maida/suji.
- Extraction rate: how much flour comes out of 100 kg wheat.
  Example: 100 kg wheat gives 75 kg flour = 75% extraction.
- Tempering / conditioning: adding water to wheat and resting it
  before milling so the bran becomes tough and the endosperm
  becomes soft. This gives better separation and cleaner flour.
- Moisture: water content in wheat or flour. Too high = spoilage
  and lumps. Too low = poor milling and low weight.
- Ash content: indicates how much bran/mineral is in flour. Lower
  ash = whiter, more refined flour.
- Gluten: protein that gives dough its stretch. Important for
  roti softness and bread/biscuit type.
- Granulation: particle size of flour.
- Starch damage: broken starch granules due to harsh grinding;
  affects water absorption of dough.

--- 2. PROCESS FLOW (simple order) ---
Step 1: Receiving and storage of wheat
Step 2: Pre-cleaning (remove big dust, straw, stones)
Step 3: Cleaning (separator, destoner, magnetic separator,
        aspirator, scourer)
Step 4: Tempering / dampening (add water, rest the wheat)
Step 5: Milling
        - Chakki (stone) mill: grinds whole wheat in one pass
        - Roller mill: Break rolls open the grain, then
          reduction rolls turn the pieces into flour
Step 6: Sifting (plansifter / sifter separates by particle size)
Step 7: Purifying (purifier cleans suji and middlings by removing
        bran particles)
Step 8: Blending / bin storage
Step 9: Packing and dispatch

--- 3. MACHINES AND WHAT THEY DO ---
- Pre-cleaner / Separator: removes dust, straw, big and small
  impurities by sieving and air.
- Destoner: removes stones and heavy particles from wheat. Protects
  the mill machines and keeps stones out of flour.
- Magnetic separator: removes iron particles (nails, wire pieces).
  Protects machines and ensures food safety.
- Aspirator: uses air to remove light impurities like husk and dust.
- Scourer / Wheat washer: cleans the surface of the grain.
- Tempering bin / dampener: adds and mixes water in wheat.
- Aata chakki (stone/ vertical or horizontal): grinds whole wheat;
  known for traditional taste, aroma, and whole wheat atta.
- Pulverizer: a high-speed grinder used for fine grinding of
  wheat, spices, pulses, and other grains. (Say "check suitability
  for your material" if unsure.)
- Roller mill (flour mill machine): uses pairs of rollers to
  gradually break wheat and separate flour, bran, and suji. Gives
  better control over quality and higher yield of refined flour.
- Plansifter: a multi-layer box sifter that separates flour by
  particle size after milling.
- Purifier: separates bran from suji/middlings by air and sieving.
- Packing machine: weighs and packs flour in bags.
- Spares: rollers, sieves, belts, bearings, stones (for chakki),
  motors.

--- 4. CHAKKI vs ROLLER MILL (simple comparison) ---
Chakki (stone): simpler, lower investment, suitable for whole wheat
  atta, local/retail mill, traditional taste. Less control over
  separating bran and flour.
Roller mill: higher investment, higher capacity, better control,
  can make atta, maida, and suji separately, more consistent
  quality, suitable for commercial scale.
Never say one is "always better". Say which suits which need.

--- 5. COMMON MILL PROBLEMS AND TYPICAL CAUSES ---
(Describe as "common causes", not guaranteed diagnosis.)
- Low flour yield: poor tempering, worn rollers or stones, wrong
  roll gap, improper sifting, wrong sieve size.
- Flour too hot / loses aroma: stones running too tight, high
  speed, poor ventilation.
- Dark or dull flour: poor wheat cleaning, too much bran in flour,
  worn sieves, low-quality wheat.
- Stones or iron pieces in flour: destoner or magnet not working
  or missing.
- Lumps in flour: high moisture in wheat or flour, poor storage.
- Inconsistent quality: wheat variety changes, no blending, machine
  settings not maintained.
- High electricity bill: worn stones/rollers, motor overload,
  idle running, poor maintenance, wrong machine size.
- Frequent breakdown: no preventive maintenance, bad bearings,
  dust accumulation, voltage fluctuation.
- Short shelf life of atta: high moisture, poor packing, warm
  storage, insects.

--- 6. GOOD PRACTICES (safe advice you may share) ---
- Clean wheat well before milling. Clean wheat means better flour
  and longer machine life.
- Test wheat moisture regularly before milling.
- Temper the wheat properly for roller milling.
- Check and replace worn sieves, belts, and bearings on schedule.
- Keep a maintenance log for each machine.
- Keep the mill area clean to avoid insects and contamination.
- Store flour in a cool, dry place away from walls and floor.
- Use proper packing with clear labelling.
- Train operators on basic settings and safety.
- Keep key spare parts in stock to avoid long downtime.
- Take samples of flour every shift and check color, granulation,
  and feel.

--- 7. REGULATIONS AND QUALITY (general only) ---
- Flour mills in India need FSSAI licence/registration for food
  business, based on size.
- Packed flour needs proper labelling as per FSSAI rules.
- Atta, maida, and suji have quality standards (moisture, ash,
  gluten, etc.).
- Fortification of wheat flour (iron, folic acid, vitamin B12)
  is promoted by FSSAI and government programs.
